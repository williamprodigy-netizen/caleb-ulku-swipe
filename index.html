What matters

The survey gate is the thing to steal

The thank-you page does not thank you. The video opens “congratulations on registering and securing your spot … but do not close this page yet because your registration isn't quite complete”, then makes a sub-one-minute survey the thing that completes it: “it's only once you've submitted the survey that your spot will be fully confirmed.” The stated reason is generous — he wants to tailor the class to the room. The bribe is concrete: “as a thank you for filling it out, I'm going to send you my 14-day local SEO ranking cheat sheet.” One screen buys him a second micro-commitment, four segmentation fields, and a legitimate reason to email. Show rate is our keystone metric and this is a working answer to it.

The $49 upsell is disclosed on the registration page, before it appears

FAQ item five is written as the prospect's own words: “Is this free? What's the catch?” The answer names the upsell outright — “there's a $49 VIP upgrade available if you want lifetime recordings and extended access, but it's entirely optional.” Pre-disclosing the OTO costs nothing and removes the bait-and-switch feeling when it lands. Every other funnel in this swipe file hides it.

The price anchor is real and checkable, not invented

“To get this kind of access to me normally, you'd have to be inside my pro community, and that runs $197 a month. But today, you're not paying $197. You're not even paying $100. Right here on this page, you can get the full VIP experience for just $49.” A three-step descent from a recurring price the prospect can go and verify, rather than a fabricated “$2,000 value”. Compare Shelby's invented $6,000 coach.

A product trial inside a $49 ticket

VIP does not just buy attention, it buys the software: “I'll give you full access for seven days to use it on a real client. If it doesn't deliver, then cancel, no drama.” The buyer is inside the tool before the class runs, which turns the class into a product demo instead of a pitch. Product-led growth bolted onto a webinar funnel.

Two confirmation videos, one for buyers and one for everyone else

The Wistia library holds VSL/CONFIRMATION (1m56s, includes the VIP tease) and VIPCONFIRMATION (1m00s, the identical script with the VIP paragraphs cut). Buyers do not get re-pitched what they already own. It cost him one re-cut and almost nobody bothers to do it.

One body copy, nine hooks — copy is settled, hooks are the test

All ~9 live ads run identical body copy and the identical headline (“Free Live Class: The New Way to Rank on Google”). Only the 27–56 second video varies. Eight launched on 4 August, one on 6 August. The same body copy appears on inactive ads dating back to 17 June, so it has survived roughly seven weeks unchanged across 160 total ads. Survival rate live is ~9 of 160 (5.6%). n=12 of the inactive set was read in full; those carried 1–9 ads per creative, which is Andromeda-style re-upload rather than fresh writing.

The whole ad argument is one sentence

“AI can't replace a real plumber. It can't fix a sink or install an HVAC system. So when someone searches for a local service, Google still has to show real local businesses.” The threat everyone else in the space is selling (AI is eating search) is reframed as the reason his niche is safe. A defensive market fact turned into an offensive positioning claim.

Proof by demonstration, not by testimonial

The central promise is a live build: “watch the AI agent rank a real business in a single session … 60 hours of work, executed in one session.” The objection block leans on it too — “not a pre-recorded case study someone cleaned up before you saw it … either it works or it doesn't.” Three 23–49 second student testimonials sit on the page but they are support, not the argument.

The origin story leads with a failure and a domestic detail

Six headline beats. The hook is not a number: “my wife and I were working so much we had to hire someone just to pick our son up before daycare locked the doors.” The first beat after quitting is getting a $3,000/month client's site de-indexed and being fired on the spot. Then $300,000 spent on training, the 2023 pivot to local, 97 clients in six months, two 7-figure agencies.

Five required fields, including a phone number, for a free class

First name, last name, email, phone (required), plus a required consent box covering both SMS and marketing email. That is real friction on a cold Meta click and he is paying it on purpose — the number is what makes an SMS reminder layer possible. We did not submit it: there is no research phone number and fabricating one routes real calls to a real stranger.

The scarcity furniture is broken on a live cold page

The sticky header reads “234 FREE Spots Left. Closing in 00D : 00H : 00M : 00S”. The spot count is hardcoded and never decrements; the countdown is frozen at zero — re-rendered four seconds apart, the digits were identical. The event date is hardcoded eight times in the markup, and the cold ad was still serving at 3pm ET on 6 August, two hours after the 1pm class had ended. Somebody is buying clicks onto a dead timer.

Built on Lovable, not a funnel builder

The og:image sits on storage.googleapis.com/gpt-engineer-file-uploads, the analytics file is ~flock.js, and the markup is Tailwind with Sentry bolted on. This is a vibe-coded page, not ClickFunnels or GoHighLevel. Form is Tally in a modal, video is Wistia, tracking is Meta pixel 26795566503388851 plus a Google Ads tag — so they buy Google as well as Meta.

The back end was recovered without opting in

The registration page preloads three downstream Wistia media by jsonp before you have given it anything: VSL/CONFIRMATION, VIP and VIPCONFIRMATION. All three were pulled at native quality and transcribed. The entire post-opt-in sequence — survey gate, $49 pitch, price anchor, bonus stack, scarcity cap — is documented here from a funnel we never entered. Worth checking on our own pages.
